## Why the spot matters more than the camera

In a good match video you can always see both goals, both goal areas and ideally all twelve court players. Any phone with a wide angle can do that if it stands in the right place. If it is too low or in a corner, even an expensive camera does not help: players hide each other, and half of the court is far away.

## Where does the camera go?

**Level with the halfway line, on the long side of the court, a few metres behind the sideline.** From there both goals are equally far away and you never need to pan. A few metres off the halfway line works too, but the middle is best.

Good spots are the stand, a gallery, a railing on the first floor or a tall tripod behind the bench. Less good are the corners and behind the goal.

## How high should the phone be?

**As high as possible, from about 3 metres.** The higher the camera, the less the players hide each other and the better you see the gaps in the defence. At eye level you mostly see the backs of the nearest players.

Without a stand, a tall tripod helps. Put it where nobody trips over it and no emergency exit is blocked, and ask the caretaker first.

## The most important step: the wide angle

A handball court is 40 metres long. From the halfway line the normal lens sees little more than one half. Almost all recent phones have a second, wider view: in the camera app it is called **"0.5×"** or **"Ultra Wide"**. With it, both goals fit in.

- **What must be in the picture:** both goals and both goal areas in full, the sidelines as far as possible. If the two near corners are missing, that is fine.
- **If it still does not fit:** higher, a few metres further back, or both. Better both goals with some margin than close and sharp.
- **Distortion at the edges:** a wide angle bends straight lines slightly at the edge of the picture. Many phones correct this themselves, on an iPhone for example with the setting "Lens Correction". Leave it on.

Why both goal areas? The analysis needs the lines of both goal areas to find the court. If they are missing, it asks you while checking.

How much space a match needs depends on the phone. Many phones show in the camera settings how much space one minute of video takes. At 1080p it is roughly 40 to 100 MB a minute, depending on the setting, so a match with half-time quickly takes 5 to 9 GB. You can work it out with the [storage calculator](../rechner/speicher).

**Settings:**

- **Wide angle:** "0.5×" or "Ultra Wide".
- **Resolution:** 1080p (Full HD). 4K adds little for analysis and fills the storage four times as fast.
- **Frames per second:** 50 or 60 if the phone can do it. Fast shots are then less blurred. 25 or 30 also work.
- **Airplane mode on:** otherwise a call can stop the recording.
- **Screen lock off,** brightness down. That saves battery.
- **No zoom, no portrait.**

## Am I allowed to film?

In short: before the match, tell the opponent, the referees and the hall, and put the [notice](../aufnahme) on the door. What applies to your team, to children and to publishing is in one place: the guide [Filming in a club: what applies?](video-im-verein-einwilligung), with a [template to print](../vorlagen/einwilligung).
