## What video analysis gives you

**You see what happened, not what you saw.** On the sideline you follow the ball, get annoyed about a whistle and coach the bench. What happens on the far side you only half catch. The next day you mostly remember the last few minutes. The video has everything.

**Your players see themselves.** "You are standing too deep" is a sentence. A scene where you can see the gap convinces. Many players see themselves from the outside for the first time and grasp a correction faster.

**Conversations get concrete.** Instead of "our defence was bad" it becomes "five of the eight goals we conceded in the first half came down the right". That also helps with your assistant coach, the goalkeeper or the club board.

**You spot patterns.** One match can be chance. If the same gap opens in three matches in a row, it is a topic for training.

## What video analysis does not give you

- **It does not replace training.** The video shows the problem. It is solved in the hall.
- **No miracles from numbers.** Percentages from a single match with few shots mislead. Always write the base: "3 of 4" says more than "75 %".
- **You do not see everything.** From one camera at the side, distances and paths are estimates. What happens behind a player stays hidden.
- **Not without time.** Even with an app you need a few minutes per match, and the video session with the team needs preparing.

## How much time does it take?

Counted honestly, for an amateur team:

| Step | Time |
|---|---|
| Set up and pack away the camera | 10 to 15 minutes |
| Watch the whole match again | 60 minutes and more |
| Analyse one question (goals, goals conceded) | about 20 minutes |
| Pick three to five scenes for the team | 5 to 10 minutes |
| Video session with the team | 10 minutes |

The biggest item is watching everything. You can skip it if you start with a question and jump straight to the goals.

## How to start

1. **Film three matches in a row,** without analysing anything yet. That way you find the best spot in your hall. Guide: [How do I film a handball match with a phone?](handballspiel-filmen)
2. **Pick one question per match,** for example "Where do the goals we concede come from?". Nothing more.
3. **Show the team three scenes,** one of them a good one. Ten minutes are enough.

Before you start, inform your team and the parents and ask for consent. What applies: [Filming in a club: consent, children, retention](video-im-verein-einwilligung).

## Who benefits most?

Teams that play and train regularly and have a coach who takes 20 minutes a week. Youth teams, if the sessions stay short and positive. Less so teams that only play every few weeks: then the pattern is missing.
