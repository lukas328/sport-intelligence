## What applies in Switzerland

**Everyone has a right to their own image.** It follows from the protection of personality in the Civil Code (Art. 28 CC), as Swiss Olympic also describes it, and applies to photos as much as to videos and live streams. On top comes the Data Protection Act (FADP), in its revised form since September 2023.

**Anyone recognisable is personal data.** The FDPIC puts it this way: as soon as the people shown are recognisable, the images are personal data. The Swiss Olympic leaflet waives consent only if neither the face nor other features, expressly including the shirt number, make someone recognisable. In a match video, practically everybody is recognisable.

**This leads to a few principles.** The FDPIC lists for associations:

- Process images only for a specific purpose that the people concerned know about.
- Only as much as that purpose needs.
- Delete or anonymise as soon as the purpose is fulfilled.

On its page about photos it also advises: when in doubt, ask the people concerned for consent.

## Analysing internally is not publishing

Whether the coaching staff analyses a match internally or scenes end up on Instagram makes a big difference.

- **Internal analysis:** only the coaching staff sees the video, the team sees single scenes. Here: inform, name the purpose, limit access, delete. We recommend consent as well.
- **Publishing** (website, social media, public links): safest with the consent of the people who are recognisable. For advertising it is always needed; for reporting an overriding interest may be enough. The FDPIC reminds that content on the internet can practically never be deleted.

Swiss Olympic states that at events not open to the public, the organiser has to tell spectators and athletes that recordings are being made. So we recommend a notice on the hall door.

## What Swiss Handball already covers

For matches the federation broadcasts with RED+, Swiss Handball has its own documents. The questions and answers of Swiss Handball and RED+ of 16 July 2025 (German) say this is aligned with the Data Protection Act and the match regulations, that notices will hang in the halls, and that the details are in an information sheet. We could not open that sheet, so we say nothing about its content. It concerns the federation's broadcasts. Whether and how it applies to your own club's recording is best asked at the federation.

## Children and young people

- **The law has no fixed age;** it depends on capacity of judgement. Anyone who has it decides for themselves. The Swiss Olympic leaflet gives roughly 15 as a rule of thumb.
- **Children:** consent is given by the parents or legal guardian.
- **In the app** the parents decide up to 16, because that is simple and cautious. That is why both the young person and the parents sign the template.

Keep consent to video separate from signing up for training. Anyone who says no still plays.

## A sample consent text

Adapt it to your club. It is a suggestion, not a reviewed form. It is also available as a sheet to sign, [ready to print](../vorlagen/einwilligung).

> **Video recordings in team [name], season [year]**
>
> Responsible: [club], [responsible person]. Stored with: [service and location, e.g. Handball Intelligence, videos in the EU].
>
> The coaching staff films matches and some training sessions to analyse them and show single scenes in training. Only the coaching staff sees the whole video. The team sees single scenes, for example by a link that expires after a while. The videos are deleted: [deadline, e.g. after 90 days].
>
> I agree that:
> - ☐ matches and training sessions with me are filmed and analysed by the coaching staff.
> - ☐ scenes with me are shown to the team.
> - ☐ scenes with me are shown publicly (website, social media).
>
> I can change any answer at any time by telling the coaching staff.
>
> Name, date, signature of the player
>
> Name, date, signature of the parents (up to 16)

## And in Germany or Austria?

In the EU the GDPR applies, and authorities do not all see it the same way. In Germany, the Baden-Württemberg authority always requires the parents' consent for team photos of minors, while the Bavarian office accepts some photos of club life showing children without consent, handled with tact, and recommends consent when in doubt. Both papers deal with photos and publishing, not with internal video analysis. The safest way is to ask for consent. The age of 16 in Art. 8 GDPR concerns online services offered directly to children, not consent to photos in a club. For Austria we found no authority guidance we could quote properly here.

## How Handball Intelligence does it

This is how the app is set up: the club asks for consent by a link in the team chat, without an account and separately for each purpose. Up to 16, the parents decide. Anyone who says no does not appear in highlights. The original video is deleted after 90 days. This applies from the launch. More on the page [Data and consent](../daten).
