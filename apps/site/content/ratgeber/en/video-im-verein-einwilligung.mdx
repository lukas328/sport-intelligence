## What applies in Switzerland

**Everyone has a right to their own image.** This is in the Civil Code (Art. 28 CC) and applies to photos as much as to videos and live streams. On top comes the Data Protection Act (FADP), in its revised form since September 2023.

**Anyone recognisable is personal data.** The FDPIC puts it this way: as soon as the people shown are recognisable, the images are personal data. Swiss Olympic points out that a shirt number alone can make someone recognisable. In a match video, practically everybody is recognisable.

**This leads to a few principles** the FDPIC lists for associations:

- Process images only for a specific purpose that the people concerned know about.
- Only as much as that purpose needs.
- Delete or anonymise as soon as the purpose is fulfilled.
- When in doubt, ask the people concerned for consent.

## Analysing internally is not publishing

Whether the coaching staff analyses a match internally or scenes end up on Instagram makes a big difference.

- **Internal analysis:** only the coaching staff sees the video, the team sees single scenes. Here: inform, name the purpose, limit access, delete. Consent is the safe way.
- **Publishing** (website, social media, public links): only with the consent of the people who are recognisable. The FDPIC reminds that content on the internet can practically never be deleted.

Swiss Olympic states that at events not open to the public, the organiser has to tell spectators and athletes that recordings are being made. That is why a notice belongs on the hall door.

## Children and young people

- **Children:** consent is given by the parents or legal guardian.
- **Young people:** anyone with capacity of judgement decides for themselves. Switzerland has no fixed age limit. The Swiss Olympic leaflet gives roughly 15 as a rule of thumb.
- **In practice:** many clubs ask the parents up to 16. That is cautious and easy to explain.

Keep consent to video separate from signing up for training. Anyone who says no still plays.

## A sample consent text

Adapt it to your club. It is a suggestion, not a reviewed form.

> **Video recordings in team [name], season [year]**
>
> The coaching staff films matches and some training sessions to analyse them and show single scenes in training. Only the coaching staff sees the whole video. The team sees single scenes, for example by a link that expires after a while. The videos are deleted at the end of the season at the latest.
>
> I agree that:
> - ☐ matches and training sessions with me are filmed and analysed by the coaching staff.
> - ☐ scenes with me are shown to the team.
> - ☐ scenes with me are shown publicly (website, social media).
>
> I can change any answer at any time by telling the coaching staff.
>
> Name, date, signature (for children: parents)

## And in Germany or Austria?

In the EU the GDPR applies, and authorities do not all see it the same way. In Germany, the Baden-Württemberg authority always requires the parents' consent for team photos of minors, while the Bavarian office accepts some photos of club life showing children without consent, handled with tact, and recommends consent when in doubt. Both papers deal with photos and publishing, not with internal video analysis. The safest way is to ask for consent. The age of 16 in Art. 8 GDPR concerns online services offered directly to children, not consent to photos in a club. For Austria we found no authority guidance we could quote properly here.

## How Handball Intelligence does it

In the app, the club asks for consent by a link in the team chat, without an account and separately for each purpose. Under 16, the parents decide. Anyone who says no does not appear in highlights. The original video is deleted after 90 days. More on the page [Data and consent](../daten).
